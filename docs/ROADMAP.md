# Build from this foundation

## First product slice — choose the experiment

Moments is recommended; Projectiles and Pythagoras remain in the product plan. Do not infer that all three must ship in the first demo, or silently remove any direction.

1. Confirm mission, success/prediction check, supported commands, device and demo duration.
2. Approve final room/robot art against the Open Space reference and Kestrel's selected 3D appearance. Retain readable measurements and small conversation windows.
3. Integrate pinned Microduck assets and one compatible controller; verify reset, approach and station interaction. Benchmark a second bot. Robot tool actuation and true grasping are distinct capabilities.
4. Connect live speech and bounded model tool calls behind the API; verify recipient capture, interruptions, errors and actual latency/costs.
5. Implement one purposeful bot-to-bot moment: a learner asks one bot to set up an experiment while another observes and reports evidence. Begin with a maximum of two bot turns, then return control. Tune limits with the owner; no ambient endless chatter.
6. Prove an end-to-end learner loop and transfer question. Add another topic only after it is reliable.

## Definition of ready to pitch

The product shows a beautiful readable lab, robots visibly doing supported tasks, grounded dialogue and one understandable collaboration. Claims distinguish simulated physics, authored motion and animated tools. A failed request, stop or reset leaves the world coherent.

## Open decisions

Opening mission; precise autonomy triggers and limits; what a meaningful collaboration teaches; role differentiation; actual physical tool mechanism; final asset provenance; performance/latency budget; API costs and youth safeguards; target device; hosting; ownership of the next tasks.
